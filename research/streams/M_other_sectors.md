# M. Other parts of the economy, for scale

Research date: 2026-09-25.  The Access chart shows five worldwide figures in gray so readers can see how filmmaking and AI video compare with much larger sources of emissions.  Rows 1, 2, and 4 count CO2 only; rows 3 and 5 count CO2e.  **P** marks a primary source and **S** a secondary one; confidence is H, M, or L.

## Notes by row

1. **Cars and motorcycles.**  IEA's WEO 2025 (Annex A, Table A.4a, "Passenger cars") gives 3,208 Mt of tailpipe CO2 for 2024, including SUVs and passenger pick-ups but not goods vans or buses.  IEA doesn't publish two- and three-wheelers separately; about 0.2 Gt (0.1–0.3) is our estimate from IEA's Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025 (buses and two/three-wheelers together about 7% of just over 6 Gt of road CO2) and BNEF (two-wheelers under 5% of road CO2).  The site's range is cars plus 0.1–0.3 Gt.  Tailpipe only: producing the fuel adds more.  Including goods vans, IEA's tracking page gives about 3.8 Gt for 2023 (not used, since vans mostly carry goods).
2. **Commercial flights.**  IATA reports 942 Mt of airline CO2 (passengers and cargo) for 2024 and 882 Mt for 2023.  ICCT's flight-by-flight model found 920 Mt for 2019, within 1% of IATA.  IEA's 1,018 Mt for 2024 covers all aviation, including non-airline and domestic military flights, and sets the high end.  Non-CO2 effects (contrails, NOx, water vapor) are excluded; the UK government's recommended central multiplier is 1.7, which would make it about 1.6 billion t CO2e.
3. **Livestock digestion (enteric fermentation).**  FAOSTAT's emissions totals give 105.3 Mt of methane in 2023, 2,947 Mt CO2e at the IPCC AR5 100-year factor (methane = 28).  The low (2.84) uses the newer AR6 factor (27.0); the high (about 3.3) scales to the top of the Global Methane Budget 2025's range across inventories.  FAO's larger "livestock" headline (about 4.3 Gt) adds manure and nitrous oxide and isn't used here.  Over 20 years instead of 100, the same methane is about 8.4 Gt CO2e.
4. **Cement.**  IEA's WEO 2025 gives 2,324 Mt for 2024 (2,409 Mt in 2023), covering the chemical process that releases CO2 from limestone plus fuel burned in the kilns, but not purchased electricity.  The chemical process alone was about 1.47 Gt in 2024 (Global Carbon Project via Our World in Data).  The Global Cement and Concrete Association's "about 7% of global CO2" (about 2.6 Gt) sets the high end.  Cement also reabsorbs roughly 0.8 Gt a year as it ages; none of these figures subtract it.
5. **Fashion and textiles.**  Studies disagree because they cover different things:
   - McKinsey and Global Fashion Agenda, *Fashion on Climate* (2020): 2.1 Gt in 2018, apparel and footwear, fiber through use (the only full value-chain estimate; used as the central figure).
   - Quantis, *Measuring Fashion* (2018): 3.99 Gt in 2016, apparel 3.29 plus footwear 0.70 (the high).
   - Ellen MacArthur Foundation (2017): 1.2 Gt in 2015, textile production only.
   - Apparel Impact Institute and WRI (2021): 1.025 Gt in 2019, apparel through garment assembly; Apparel Impact Institute (2025): 0.944 Gt in 2023 (the low).
   - UNEP (2023): 2–8% of global emissions.
