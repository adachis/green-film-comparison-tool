# K. Global film and TV production footprint

Research date: 2026-09-25
Stream: K (global production footprint, all film and TV production worldwide)

## Conventions

- Units: t = metric tonnes CO2e; kt = 1,000 t; Mt = 1,000,000 t.  $ = US dollars (nominal); $M = million US dollars.
- Exchange rates (ASSUMPTION, 2024 annual averages): $1.278 per £1 and $1.082 per €1.
- Reference year: 2024.  Inputs from 2019–2025 are flagged where used.
- Tags: P = primary (the body that measured or published the data); S = secondary.  Confidence: H, M, or L.  DERIVED = our calculation, arithmetic shown.  ASSUMPTION = a parameter no source provides.
- Production boundary: development and prep through shooting and post-production delivery, as captured by production carbon calculators (fuel for generators and vehicles, grid energy for stages, offices, and edit suites, travel, accommodation, food, materials, waste, and freight).  Excluded: distribution, marketing, streaming and data delivery, broadcasting, cinema operations, audience travel, corporate overhead of studios and broadcasters, sports-rights fees, and (in nearly every benchmark) VFX and animation rendering.
- Source wording is paraphrased with exact figures and page locators (PDF page numbers unless noted).
- Final results are rounded to two significant figures; intermediate values are not rounded.

## Summary of findings

- Reconciled estimate for 2024: **about 3.5 Mt CO2e a year (low 2.0 Mt, high 6.5 Mt)** for all film and TV production worldwide, on a boundary comparable to BAFTA albert, the Sustainable Entertainment Alliance (SEA) figures with a 1.3× uplift, and Carbon'Clap.
- The four methods agree reasonably well: spend × intensity 3.2 Mt (1.7–6.1); volume × per-title and per-hour footprints 3.5 Mt (1.8–6.7); UK scale-up 3.7 Mt (2.3–5.9); Netflix share 5.3 Mt (3.2–8.5), which leans high because Netflix counts more categories than the calculators do.
- By segment (central): feature films ≈ 1.0 Mt, scripted TV ≈ 1.3 Mt, and unscripted, factual, entertainment, children's, news, and sport ≈ 1.2 Mt.  Scripted film and TV ≈ 2.3 Mt (1.3–4.0).
- Compared with the site's AI figure (0.6 Mt): about 5.8× at the central values (AI ≈ 17% of production), 3.3×–11× across the production range, and 0.8×–39× across the AI range.
- No credible published global production total exists.  The UCLA 2006 figures (8 Mt Los Angeles, 8.4 Mt California, 15 Mt US) measure something different and are often misquoted.  Several widely repeated "statistics" have no traceable source.
- Advertising production (separate): about 1 Mt (0.3–2.5 Mt), low confidence.
- The biggest uncertainties are global production spend ($130B–$220B), differences in what each calculator counts (radiative forcing, materials, spend-based factors), and the near absence of measured footprints outside the UK, the US studio system, and France.

**Corrections this stream made to figures already on the site (verified against the source PDFs):**

- albert's 174,437 t is not "all UK film and TV production."  The report says its data "likely represents the majority of UK TV productions" but doesn't capture company operations "or the majority of UK based feature films" (PDF p. 7).  The site now labels it "UK TV production, 2024."
- SEA's Tentpole+ films averaged 2,962 t in 2023–24 (p. 5).  The 2,996 t the site used as the top of the tentpole range is the 2020–22 average.
- SEA's 128.6 t per one-hour episode uses the same four-category scope as its tentpole figures, so the calculator's 1.3× uplift applies to it too (about 167 t).  The calculator now says so under the shoot figure.
- Carbon'Clap's 271 t fiction-feature average comes from features with an average budget of €7.0M, above a typical French feature, so it may overstate a low-budget indie.

**Caution on albert's per-hour figure.**  If 15.33 t per hour were total emissions divided by total hours, 2024 would have 174,437 ÷ 15.33 = 11,379 hours (DERIVED).  But 268,168 hours over four years averages about 67,000 hours a year.  So the headline is most likely an average of per-production intensities.  The total-divided-by-hours figure would be about 174,437 ÷ 67,000 ≈ 2.6 t per hour (DERIVED; L–M).  Multiplying global TV hours by 15.3 t per hour would overstate the total about fivefold.  By contrast, Carbon'Clap's 16 t per hour is total divided by total duration, but its sample is dominated by fiction and documentaries.

## Scope and caveats

- **Included:** production-phase emissions as counted by calculators, from prep through delivery: generator and vehicle fuel, grid power, flights, road, rail, hotels, food, set and costume materials, waste, freight, and edit and grade suites.  Post-production is small in these tools: about 2% of albert feature footprints, and SEA counts none.
- **Excluded:** distribution, marketing, streaming and data delivery, broadcast transmission, cinemas, audience travel, studio and broadcaster overhead, sports-rights fees, and creator and online video production (large, unmeasured, and outside "film and TV").
- **VFX and animation rendering is excluded from nearly every benchmark.**  One extreme example: *Avatar: The Way of Water* used 3.3 billion thread-hours of cloud rendering, about 8–28 GWh and 4,800–16,200 t on Australia's grid (streams D2 and L), against 2,444 t for an average tentpole's physical production.  Globally, 1–5 million threads rendering year-round × 3–6 Wh × 0.3–0.5 kg per kWh ≈ 0.01–0.13 Mt a year (ASSUMPTION; L), or up to about 4% of the central total.  For the few VFX-heavy tentpoles each year, the per-film figure could be understated severalfold.
- **Calculators count different things.**  albert now applies a 1.7× RF multiplier to flights; SEA does not, so RF alone adds about 12–20% to flight-heavy footprints.  SEA counts four categories (the site's 1.3× uplift matches albert's category mix).  Carbon'Clap and Netflix count more, partly with spend-based factors.  Most data are self-reported and unaudited.
- Counting by origin versus by filming location doesn't change the global total, but co-productions may be counted in more than one country's film tallies.
- News, talk, and live sport production are inside "all TV production."  For comparisons with generative video, the scripted subtotal (about 2.3 Mt) is the closer like-for-like figure.

## Gaps

- No global dataset of production footprints exists, and there is no authoritative 2024 figure for global production spend.  Olsberg's last global estimate is for 2019, and Ampere's totals include sports rights and licensing.
- There are no global counts of scripted TV hours.  European Audiovisual Observatory and Ampere detail is blocked or paywalled, and the volumes for China, India, Latin America, and Türkiye are assumptions.
- albert does not publish its hours denominator or define its per-hour metric.  Its footprint counts are internally inconsistent.
- Unscripted, news, and sport footprints outside the UK, and productions in low-cost markets (India, Nigeria, Southeast Asia) where diesel generators are heavily used, are almost unmeasured.
- Ad production has no credible global spend figure.  AdGreen's sample is mostly UK and self-selected.
- Some figures come from search-result excerpts because the sites blocked automated retrieval: Ofcom's £3.5bn, FX's 516, the UK and global ad-spend totals, and UNESCO's 7,610.  They feed the method tables but none is shown on the site.
