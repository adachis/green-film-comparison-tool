# Stream E: Dollar cost of conventional production (micro-budget to tentpole, including VFX) and the real cost of AI production

Research date: 2026-09-25. All values are USD unless marked £ (GBP) or CA$ (CAD).

**Conventions used in this document**

- **P / S**: P means a primary source (a union rate sheet, a government or FilmLA document, a company filing, or a vendor price list). S means secondary (trade press, payroll companies, agency blogs, aggregators, or Wikipedia summarising press reports).
- **Confidence**: H = high (primary, or several consistent secondaries). M = medium (one credible secondary). L = low (a marketing blog, an unverified claim, or my own derivation built on assumptions).
- **Derived** means my own arithmetic on sourced inputs. The inputs and any assumptions are stated each time.
- **Unverified** means a claim I found repeated in secondary sources but could not trace to a primary source.
- **Research limit.** The session's WebSearch quota (200 calls, shared across streams) ran out partway through this stream. After that I used only direct fetches of known URLs: union PDFs, FilmLA, Wikipedia raw pages, vendor price lists, and site sitemaps. Section 4 lists the items that were left unverified or not found because of this.

## 1. Summary of key findings

1. **Cost per finished minute runs across more than four orders of magnitude.** Real 2015–2026 budgets divided by runtime (derived) give:
   - Scrappy micro-features: about $100–$10,000/min. Tangerine is $1.1k/min, Monsters $5.3k/min and Searching $8.6k/min.
   - Indie features in the $2M–$6M range: about $15k–$45k/min. Get Out, Anora and Terrifier 3 fall here.
   - Mid-budget films in the $12M–$100M range: about $110k–$660k/min. Examples are Past Lives, Conclave, Weapons, Civil War, Oppenheimer and Sinners.
   - Studio tentpoles in the $150M–$430M range: about **$1.3M–$3.4M/min**. F1 is at the low end and Deadpool & Wolverine at the high end ($429M net / 128 min). The typical tentpole is about $1.7M–$2.5M/min.
2. **The cost per shoot day comes from primary California Film Commission data** (qualified in-state spend, which excludes most above-the-line costs and out-of-state VFX), published in FilmLA's Scripted Content Study:
   - TV series: an average of **$479k/day** (2021) and **$582k/day** (2022). The range runs from about $320k/day (Chad, a half-hour) to $1.14M/day (Westworld S4).
   - Features: an average of **$760k/day** (2021) and **$929k/day** (2022). Everything Everywhere All at Once was $315k/day; The Gray Man was $1.64M/day.
3. **Scripted TV per episode.**
   - Network one-hour dramas: about **$3.8M–$4.2M** qualified spend per episode (This Is Us S6, S.W.A.T.).
   - Cable and streaming one-hours: **$4.2M–$15M** (Mayans, The Dropout, Picard, Euphoria, Westworld).
   - Premium franchise streaming: **$20M–$58M** per episode (House of the Dragon, Andor S2, Stranger Things 5, The Rings of Power), reported.
   - A half-hour single-camera show (Chad S2): about $1.4M per episode.
4. **Published budget breakdowns (primary: FilmLA 2016).**
   - A $295.5M tentpole spent **$60M on VFX (20%)**, $64.4M above the line (22%), $141M on production, and $85.5M on all post including VFX (29%).
   - A $55M film spent 30% above the line, $6.73M (12%) on special photographic effects, and about 1% on insurance.
   - Stephen Follows' set of 107 budgets gives shares of ATL 16–26%, production 56–74%, post 7–13% and other 1–8%. Insurance falls to about 1% on larger films.
5. **2026 labor is more expensive.** The 2026 SAG-AFTRA TV/Theatrical deal was ratified in June 2026 and runs from 7/1/2026 to 6/30/2030.
   - It adds 3% a year. The day performer rate goes to **$1,283** and weekly to **$4,456**. Background actors go to **$231/day**.
   - Pension and health rises to **22%** (21.5% for background) from 9/6/2026.
   - Low-budget day rates for 2026–27: Ultra Low Budget $256.60, Moderate Low Budget $449.05, Low Budget $833.95.
   - IATSE Local 600 (the 2024–27 Basic Agreement, 8/2/2026–7/31/2027): a DP earns **$150.54/hr ($1,204.32 per 8-hour day)**.
   - The IATSE–AICP commercial scale for LA (10/30/2025) puts a key grip on a 12-hour day at about $926 and a DP at about $2,160 in wages.
   - The DGA's high-budget feature director minimum is **$25,214/week**.
   - The DGA, WGA and SAG-AFTRA all signed new four-year deals in 2026, with AI provisions.
6. **Equipment is cheap next to labor.** An LA rental house lists:
   - ARRI **Alexa 35 body/production set at $1,000/day ($3,000/week)**, RED V-Raptor at $900/day and Sony FX6 at $295/day.
   - A 1-ton grip/electric van at $400–450/day.
   - Drone packages at **$2,500–$8,500/day**. The $2,500 package includes the Part 107 pilot, visual observer and $5M aviation liability cover.
   - FilmLA permits: a **$931 application fee**, a $232 notification fee, and $78 administration fees each for explosions, drones and helicopters. An LAFD fire safety officer costs **$127/hr** (4-hour minimum); an LA County FSO costs **$230.19/hr**.
7. **Scenario costs.**
   - **1,000 SAG-AFTRA background actors cost about $281k per day** in wages plus P&H alone (2026–27), before meals, wardrobe, holding areas or extra crew (derived). Spectre hired 1,500 extras and replicated them digitally to look like about 10,000.
   - The labor and permit core of an explosion gag is only about **$10–11k per day**: stunt coordinator, two stunt performers, a pyro foreperson, two effects technicians, an FSO and permits (derived from union and FilmLA rates). The real cost drivers are the vehicle, rigging, and the **extra unit days**, which run at the $300k–$1.6M/day rates above on studio-scale shows.
   - Top Gun: Maverick paid the US Navy **$11,374 per flight hour** for F/A-18s.
   - Spectre's car costs were an estimated **£24M ($48M)**.
8. **VFX.**
   - The VFX share of a tentpole budget was 20% (FilmLA 2016) and 32% (Spider-Man 2, $65M of $202M). VFX Voice quotes 20–25% of production costs for streaming shows.
   - Shot counts on recent tentpoles are 1,500–2,200 (Dune: Part Two 2,156; Superman 1,766). That implies an **average of about $27k–$40k per shot** at tentpole scale (derived from the $60M VFX line).
   - Low-cost counter-examples exist: Godzilla Minus One did 610 shots with 35 artists on a total film budget of about $10–15M, and Monsters did 250 shots by one person on $500k.
   - **Per-shot vendor bid price tiers could not be sourced** after the search quota ran out (Section 4).
9. **What AI production actually costs.**
   - **Hell Grind (Higgsfield, Cannes market 2026)**: a 95-minute feature for **$500k, of which $400k (80%) was compute**. It took 15 people two weeks and generated more than 16,000 clips for 253 final shots, which works out to **about $5.3k per finished minute** (derived).
   - **Kalshi NBA Finals spot (2025)**: $2,000 in generation cost, one person, 2–3 days, and 300–400 generations for 15 usable clips. Production-company quotes for the same spot were six to seven figures.
   - **Coca-Cola's 2025 holiday spots**: about 100 people across the brand, WPP and two AI studios, including 5 AI specialists at Silverside who generated more than 70,000 clips. It took about a month instead of a year. The cost was not disclosed.
   - **Staircase Studios** makes features for less than $500k. **Critterz** is an AI-assisted animated feature for less than $30M in 9 months.
   - **Netflix (primary, Q2 2026 letter)**: generative AI was used in about 300 titles in 2026, "at a lower cost than traditional methods". The co-CEO reportedly said The American Experiment's 17 minutes of AI-enhanced footage took half the time at half the cost (secondary).
10. **When AI saves money and when it doesn't.**
    - AI's cost per minute ($50–$1,000/min for DIY compute; about $5k/min for a professionally made feature; $2.5k–$20k per :30 from AI agencies; up to a reported $100k–$235k per spot from celebrity AI directors) is **orders of magnitude below** tentpole, TV-drama and national-commercial live action ($90k–$3.4M/min). The gap is widest for crowds, explosions, period worlds and aerials.
    - AI is **not clearly cheaper** than scrappy conventional work: Hell Grind's $5.3k/min is the same as Monsters and more than Tangerine ($1.1k/min). It is also not clearly cheaper than a one-day talking-head or corporate shoot ($1k–$5k per finished minute), or UGC creators (about $198 per deliverable).
    - Hidden costs are human iteration (20–60+ generations per kept shot), the loss of copyright protection for "prompts alone" (US Copyright Office, Jan 2025), SAG-AFTRA commercials digital-replica fees of **1.5× the session fee**, and EU AI Act deepfake disclosure (Art. 50, from 2 Aug 2026).

### 2.12 Hidden costs of AI content: legal, insurance and union

- **Copyright (US, P, H):** The Copyright Office's Part 2 report (29 Jan 2025) says: "Copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material, or material where there is insufficient human control over the expressive elements." It also says "prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users of an AI system the authors of the output" [S77]. The practical cost is weaker IP protection, plus extra human authorship work and documentation to secure it.
- **SAG-AFTRA 2025 Commercials Contracts (S, H):**
  - Using a digital replica costs **1.5× the scale session fee** per commercial, plus use and holding fees.
  - It requires 48-hour notice and written consent.
  - A synthetic performer that replaces a human principal triggers 1.5 session fees plus P&H contributions. Davis+Gilbert says synthetic performers are acceptable only if cost saving is not the primary motive [S6, S7].
- **The 2026 DGA deal** reaffirms that directors' work "must continue to be performed by a person". It gives directors control over AI-generated footage and funds a skills program [S15].
- **The 2026 SAG-AFTRA TV/Theatrical deal** includes AI protections. The specifics were not retrievable (the SAG-AFTRA and trade sites were blocked or paywalled).
- **EU AI Act, Article 50 (P, H):** From **2 Aug 2026**, deployers must disclose AI-generated or manipulated deepfake content. For evidently artistic or fictional works, the duty is limited to a disclosure that "does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work" [S78].
- **E&O insurance and clearance:** I found **no sourced premium or exclusion data** (gap). The Staircase and Critterz models keep human writers, voices and artists partly to preserve chain of title.

## 3. Methodology notes: the bottom-up cost model

### 3.1 Model structure (conventional)

```
Total = ATL + Σ_depts( crew_count × days × day_rate × (1 + fringe) )
      + cast_days × SAG rate × (1 + P&H 21–22%) + BG_count × days × $231 × 1.215
      + equipment (camera, G&E, grip truck, generator) × days
      + locations (fees + permits + FSO/police) + stage days
      + travel/per diem + transport (Teamsters hours) + catering
      + art/construction/wardrobe (scenario-dependent) + stunts/SFX
      + post (edit, sound, music) + VFX (shots × avg cost/shot)
      + insurance (~0.5–1.1%) + contingency (~10% BTL) [+ AICP markup 20–35% for commercials]
```

- **Per finished minute** = Total ÷ screen minutes. The pace benchmarks are: network hour-long TV about 5.4 finished minutes per shoot day; premium streaming about 2.5–4.6 (derived from CFC days and episode runtimes). Features are slower (for example, EEAAO's 139 minutes over about 39 CA days is about 3.6 min/day).
- **Per shoot day** anchors (qualified CA spend): TV $479k–$582k; features $760k–$929k [S30]. For all-in totals, add ATL above the qualified caps plus out-of-state VFX. The FilmLA tentpole shows VFX and post at 29% of the total.

### 3.2 Worked example: union commercial crew day (LA, IATSE–AICP 10/30/2025, 12-hour day)

- **Hourly crew cost for a 12-hour day** = 8 × rate + 4 × 1.5 × rate = 14 × the hourly rate. On-call department heads get a flat daily rate.
- **A 36-person IATSE crew** costs **$34,890** in wages. That crew is:
  - DP, operator, 2 × 1st AC, 2 × 2nd AC and a DIT
  - Mixer and boom
  - Key grip, 2nd grip, dolly grip and 3 grips
  - Gaffer, best boy and 3 lighting technicians
  - Prop master and 2nd prop
  - Key costumer and assistant
  - Makeup and hair heads, plus a makeup artist
  - Script supervisor, lead set dresser, 2 set dressers, craft service and first aid
  - Production designer, costume designer and set decorator (on-call)
- **Fringes** at an assumed 25–35% bring it to **about $43.6k–$47.1k**.
- **Other sourced components:**
  - 4 SAG principals: 4 × $783.10 × 1.235 = about $3.9k.
  - Two Alexa 35 bodies: $2k (lenses not sourced).
  - FilmLA permit, notification, spot check, FSO for 13 hours and police for 10 hours: about $3.8k.
  - Production-company markup: 20–35%.
- **Sourced floor:** about **$55k–$60k per shoot day**, before director and producer fees, lighting and grip trucks, locations, catering, art department and post. A realistic all-in figure for a national-level spot is **$75k–$150k per shoot day**. This is a derived estimate (L).

### 3.3 Worked example: non-union indie crew day (mid-sized US market)

- A 10-person core crew at median rates costs **$6,750/day**: DP $1,200, 1st AD $1,000, sound $750, gaffer $700, key grip $650, 1st AC $700, production designer $1,000, and 3 PAs at $250 [S21].
- Add kit fees ($300–$750 for sound), camera ($295–$1,000/day), a 1-ton G&E van ($400–$450) and cast.
- On a ULB feature: SAG $256.60/day per performer plus 22% P&H.
- A realistic indie day is roughly $15k–$50k (derived). Atlanta is 0.85–0.90× and NYC 0.95–1.0× of these rates.

### 3.4 AI production cost model (for the comparison tool)

```
AI_total = compute (Σ generations × seconds × $/s)            # Stream C/F price data
         + human labor (hours × rate)                          # dominant for short-form
         + tools/subscriptions + upscaling + TTS/music
         + legal/clearance/disclosure + (union fees if replicas/synthetic performers)
         + agency margin (44–82% gross margin reported for AI agencies, L)
```

**Calibration points:**

- **Iteration ratio:**
  - 20–27 generations per kept clip (Kalshi)
  - about 63 (Hell Grind: 16,000 clips for 253 shots)
  - 3–10 (Curious Refuge's Seedance hit rate)
  - 20–30 for skilled vs 200–300 for beginners (course marketer, L)
- **Share of cost that is compute:** 80% on Hell Grind (a long-form feature with a small team). For short-form commercial work, labor usually dominates: editing is $100 of a $229.80 60-s ad (L), and a senior agency spends 25–40 hours per job.
- **Team size and time:**
  - Kalshi: 1 person, 2–3 days
  - Hell Grind: 15 people, 2 weeks
  - Genre: a pod of about 5 roles, about 1 month
  - Coca-Cola: about 100 people in total, 5 of them AI specialists, about 1 month

### 3.5 Break-even logic (when AI saves money and when it doesn't)

- **Clear AI savings (1,000×-plus):** spectacle-heavy content where conventional costs $0.5M–$3.4M per finished minute. That covers crowds (about $281k/day for 1,000 background actors), explosions and extra unit days ($0.3M–$1.6M/day), period worlds ($19M set construction on a tentpole), and aerials ($11,374/hr for jets).
- **Moderate AI savings (5–100×):** national and regional commercials ($0.3M–$1.5M per finished minute conventionally vs $5k–$235k per spot for AI), music videos and explainers.
- **Little or no AI saving:**
  - Scrappy micro-budget narrative: Tangerine $1.1k/min and Monsters $5.3k/min, vs Hell Grind $5.3k/min.
  - Talking-head and corporate work ($1k–$5k per finished minute; a 1-day shoot delivers many minutes).
  - UGC-style social ($50–$500 per deliverable).
  - Anything needing real people, real products, real events or union talent. SAG-AFTRA replica and synthetic-performer fees, copyright limits and EU disclosure rules add cost and risk here.
